Forecasting South Africa’s hourly electricity demand

Python pandas scikit-learn XGBoost Time series 2026

I built models that forecast South Africa’s national electricity demand 24 hours ahead from 40,248 hours of Eskom data (April 2022 to November 2026), and compared them with Eskom’s own published forecast. The best model, XGBoost, matched Eskom’s forecast overall and was 14% more accurate at the evening peak, when the grid is under the most pressure.

What I found

  • Demand peaks on winter evenings: 30.4 GW at 18:00 on average, against 26.9 GW in summer.
  • Weekends are 6% and public holidays 9% below weekday demand.
  • Typical daily demand fell from about 26–27 GW in 2022 to 22 GW in 2026, consistent with the growth of rooftop solar.
  • Demand at the same hour one week earlier was the most important predictor.
  • Training only on data after heavy load shedding (from April 2024) helped XGBoost and the linear model.

How I did it

  1. Cleaned the Eskom data: parsed 12-hour timestamps, checked for duplicates, gaps and impossible values, and removed forecast-only rows.
  2. Explored daily, weekly and seasonal patterns, holidays and load shedding.
  3. Built features: calendar and holiday flags, Fourier terms for the daily, weekly and yearly cycles, and lagged demand.
  4. Compared naive baselines, Fourier regression (least squares), Random Forest and XGBoost against Eskom’s forecast, using MAE, RMSE, MAPE and evening-peak MAE.

Predicting credit card default for fair, cost-based lending

Python scikit-learn XGBoost Credit risk Classification 2026

I built models that predict whether a credit card client will miss their next payment, using 29,965 clients of a Taiwanese bank, and turned the predictions into an approve-or-decline rule based on the cost of each type of error. The best model, XGBoost, caught 74% of defaulters, cut the default rate among approved clients from 22.1% to 10.1% and halved the cost of approving everyone, without needing sex, age or marital status.

What I found

  • The number of months with a late payment was by far the strongest predictor of default.
  • Clients two months late on their latest payment defaulted 69% of the time, against 13–17% for clients who paid on time.
  • The default rate fell from 32% for the lowest credit limits to 11% for the highest.
  • After correcting for class weighting, the probabilities were well calibrated, so the best cut-off followed directly from the cost ratio.
  • Removing sex, age and marital status changed the AUC by only 0.0005, and the model then treated men, women and age groups similarly.

How I did it

  1. Cleaned the data: removed duplicates and merged undocumented education and marital status codes.
  2. Built features for credit utilisation, months late and average payment ratio.
  3. Compared logistic regression, random forest and XGBoost, tuned by 5-fold cross-validation, using AUC, Gini, KS, Brier score and calibration.
  4. Chose a cut-off that minimises the expected cost of lending decisions, and checked fairness across sex and age groups.
